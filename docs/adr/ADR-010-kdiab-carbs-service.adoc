= ADR-010: kdiab-carbs Dedicated Food and Carbohydrate Service
:toc: left
:icons: font

* *Status*: Accepted
* *Date*: 2026-05-14
* *Deciders*: triplem

== Context

Carbohydrate data in a T1D management platform has two distinct concerns:

1. *Food catalog*: a database of food items with their carbohydrate content per 100 g (or per
   serving). Entries are created once and reused across many meals. They may be shared across
   users or user-private. Updates are infrequent and driven by curation, not by clinical events.

2. *Carb entries*: a log of carbohydrates actually consumed at a given time, linked to a food
   item and quantity. Entries are immutable after recording and form part of the patient's
   treatment history.

The original design placed carb entries inside `kdiab-treatments` alongside bolus, basal, and
correction events. This created several problems:

* *Domain leakage*: `kdiab-treatments` models clinical treatment events (insulin delivery,
  corrections). Carbohydrate consumption is input to dosing decisions, not a treatment action.
  Mixing them inflated the treatment model with food catalog concepts.
* *Access patterns*: the food catalog is queried for autocomplete and browsing (read-heavy,
  paginated, searchable by name). Treatment entries are appended and retrieved by time range
  (append-only, time-series). A single service cannot optimise indexes for both patterns.
* *Ownership and lifecycle*: a food item in the catalog has an independent lifecycle — it can
  be created, updated, or soft-deleted without affecting recorded carb entries that reference it.
  Embedding the catalog in `kdiab-treatments` would require either tight coupling or denormalising
  food names into every treatment row.
* *Growth path*: the food catalog is a candidate for enrichment with glycaemic index, macros, and
  integration with external food databases (USDA, OpenFoodFacts). This growth should not force
  changes to the treatment service's schema or API.

== Decision

Create `kdiab-carbs` as a dedicated microservice that owns both the food catalog and carb
entry tracking. `kdiab-treatments` no longer stores carb data; when a meal bolus is recorded,
`kdiab-treatments` stores the bolus event and `kdiab-carbs` stores the corresponding carb entry.

=== Domain model

[cols="2,3"]
|===
| Entity | Description

| `FoodItem`   | A food or ingredient entry: name, carbs per 100 g, optional serving size, userId (null = shared)
| `CarbEntry`  | A recorded consumption event: userId, timestamp, foodItemId, quantityGrams, carbsGrams (computed)
|===

=== API surface

[source]
----
GET    /api/v1/food-items          List food items (paginated, searchable)
POST   /api/v1/food-items          Create a food item
GET    /api/v1/food-items/{id}     Get a food item by ID
PUT    /api/v1/food-items/{id}     Update a food item
DELETE /api/v1/food-items/{id}     Soft-delete a food item

GET    /api/v1/users/{userId}/carb-entries       List carb entries (by time range)
POST   /api/v1/users/{userId}/carb-entries       Record a carb entry
GET    /api/v1/users/{userId}/carb-entries/{id}  Get a carb entry by ID
DELETE /api/v1/users/{userId}/carb-entries/{id}  Delete a carb entry
----

=== Service coordinates

* Port: `8085`
* Audience: `carbs` (JWT audience claim required for auth)
* Database: `kdiab-carbs` PostgreSQL database
* OpenAPI spec: `kdiab-carbs/api/openapi.yaml`

=== Integration with kdiab-analyze

The `kdiab-analyze` BFF aggregates carb data for the analytics dashboard (daily carb totals,
carb-to-dose ratio). It calls `kdiab-carbs` via its HTTP client alongside `kdiab-measures`,
`kdiab-treatments`, and `kdiab-profiles`. The `kdiab-analyze-frontend` Keycloak client was
updated with a `carbs` audience mapper so the forwarded JWT is accepted by `kdiab-carbs`.

== Consequences

=== Positive

* `kdiab-treatments` models only clinical treatment events; its domain is clean and focused.
* The food catalog can evolve independently — new fields, external integrations, and search
  enhancements do not affect the treatment service.
* Separate indexes optimised for each access pattern: full-text search on `FoodItem.name`;
  B-tree on `CarbEntry.userId, timestamp` for time-range queries.
* Soft-delete on food items preserves referential integrity: historic carb entries continue to
  reference deleted food items without cascading side-effects.

=== Negative

* Recording a meal bolus now requires two writes: one to `kdiab-treatments` (bolus event) and
  one to `kdiab-carbs` (carb entry). The frontend is responsible for both calls; there is no
  distributed transaction. A partial failure (bolus written, carb entry not written, or vice
  versa) must be handled by the client with a retry.
* The `kdiab-analyze` BFF makes one additional HTTP call per analytics request to aggregate
  carb data from `kdiab-carbs`.
* An additional Liquibase container (`liquibase-carbs`) and a separate PostgreSQL database
  (`kdiab-carbs`) are required in `docker-compose.yml`.

=== Neutral

* `kdiab-carbs` uses the same Kotlin + Ktor + Exposed ORM + Liquibase + hexagonal architecture
  as `kdiab-measures`, `kdiab-profiles`, and `kdiab-treatments`.
* `kdiab-carbs` depends on `kdiab-common` for shared domain types and Ktor plugins.

== Alternatives Considered

=== Embed carb data in kdiab-treatments (status quo)

Store carb entries alongside bolus and correction events in `kdiab-treatments`. *Rejected*:
conflates two different domain concepts (clinical treatment events vs. food intake records),
creates mixed access patterns within one database, and blocks independent evolution of the
food catalog.

=== Embed food catalog in kdiab-measures

Add food items as a new measurement type in `kdiab-measures`. *Rejected*: food items are not
health measurements. They are a reference dataset used as input to dosing decisions. Placing
them in a measurements service would be a category error.

=== Use kdiab-treatments for carb entries and a separate catalog service

Keep carb entries in `kdiab-treatments` and extract only the food catalog to a new service.
*Rejected*: the carb entries themselves have a different lifecycle and access pattern from
treatment events and benefit from being co-located with the food catalog they reference.
Splitting across two services while keeping entries in `kdiab-treatments` would recreate the
access-pattern mismatch and add a cross-service foreign key.
