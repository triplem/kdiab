= kdiab Patient User Guide
:toc: left
:toc-title: Contents
:icons: font
:imagesdir: images/patient-guide
:experimental:

kdiab is your personal Type 1 Diabetes (T1D) management platform.
It brings together your CGM readings, insulin treatments, and analytics in one place, and lets you share data securely with your healthcare team.

[NOTE]
====
This guide covers the English interface at 1280 × 900 desktop resolution.
Mobile layout may differ.
====

== Part I — Getting Started

This part explains how to reach the application, log in, and understand the main screen layout.

=== Logging In

. Open your web browser and navigate to the kdiab URL provided by your clinic.
. You will see the kdiab welcome screen.
+
image::login-page.png[kdiab login screen showing the app welcome message and Log in button, width=800]
. Click the *Log in* button.
  kdiab uses your clinic's Keycloak identity provider — you will be redirected to a secure login page.
. Enter your *username* and *password*, then click *Sign In*.
. After a successful login you are returned to the kdiab dashboard automatically.

[TIP]
====
If you do not have an account, ask your treating physician or clinic administrator.
Self-registration requires an invitation from a doctor or admin.
====

=== Creating an Account

New patients must be invited by a doctor or an administrator.
Once the invitation is accepted, you receive an e-mail with a link to set your password through Keycloak.
After that, use the standard *Log in* flow above.

As a patient you will not see this banner on your own account.

== Part II — Daily Workflow

This part covers the screens you will use every day to record and review your data.

=== Dashboard

The Dashboard is the first screen after login.
It summarises your most recent glucose reading, trends, and device status at a glance.

image::dashboard-overview.png[Full dashboard view showing glucose hero tile, IOB/COB summary, device status, and CGM trend chart, width=800]

==== Glucose Hero Tile

image::dashboard-hero-tile.png[Current glucose reading 144 mg/dL with downward arrow trend and delta values, width=800]

The large tile at the top shows:

* *Current reading* — the most recent CGM value in your preferred unit (mg/dL or mmol/L).
* *Trend arrow* — direction and speed of change (↑ Rising, → Stable, ↓ Falling, etc.).
* *Δ (delta)* — change since the previous reading (5-minute delta).
* *Δ15* — change over the last 15 minutes.
* *Age* — how many minutes ago the reading was recorded.
  A warning appears if the reading is more than 30 minutes old.

==== IOB / COB / Basal Summary

Below the hero tile, a row of cards shows:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Value | Meaning

| *IOB* — Insulin On Board
| Estimated active insulin remaining from previous boluses (units)

| *COB* — Carbs On Board
| Estimated active carbohydrates from previous meal entries (grams)

| *Basal*
| Your currently scheduled basal rate from the active profile (U/h)

| *DIA*
| Duration of Insulin Action from the active profile
|===

==== Device Status Widget

The Device Status card shows live data pushed from your APS or CGM app (AAPS, xDrip+, Juggluco):

* Device name and version
* Pump model
* Reservoir level and age
* Pump battery
* Sensor age and expected expiry
* Cannula / infusion site age

This widget is populated by the Nightscout compatibility layer.
If it shows no data, ensure your device app is configured to upload to the kdiab Nightscout endpoint (see <<_nightscout_device_integration>>).

image::dashboard-cgm-chart.png[CGM trend chart showing glucose readings over the selected time window with treatments overlaid, width=800]

The chart plots CGM readings over time.
Coloured zones show the clinical ranges:

|===
| Zone | mg/dL | mmol/L

| Very Low (red)
| < 54
| < 3.0

| Low (orange)
| 54–70
| 3.0–3.9

| Target (green)
| 70–180
| 3.9–10.0

| High (yellow)
| 180–250
| 10.0–13.9

| Very High (red)
| > 250

Treatment markers appear on the chart:

* *●* — BGM fingerstick reading
* *▲* — Bolus dose
* *●* — Carbohydrate entry
* *⊕* — Cannula / infusion site change
* *◆* — Sensor insert

Use the time-window buttons (2h, 4h, 6h, 12h, 24h) to zoom in or out, and the *←* / *→* arrows to move to an earlier or later window.

==== Basal Rate Chart

image::dashboard-basal-chart.png[Basal insulin rate chart showing scheduled vs. delivered basal, width=800]

Below the CGM chart, the Basal Rate chart shows:

* *Scheduled* — the planned basal rate from your active profile (grey line)
* *Over profile / Under profile / Interrupted* — actual rates delivered, coloured to show temporary rate changes

=== Measures

The Measures tab lists all recorded health measurements.
It covers CGM readings, BGM fingerstick values, blood pressure, weight, and pulse.

==== Adding a Manual Measurement

. Click the *+ Measures* button.
+
image::add-measure-modal.png[Add Measurement modal showing type selector, date/time picker, and glucose value field, width=700]
. Select the measurement *Type* (e.g. Blood Glucose (Manual)).
. Set the *Measured At* date and time.
. Enter the value (e.g. blood glucose in the unit shown).
. Click *Save*.

==== Measures List

image::measure-list.png[Measures list showing CGM and BGM readings in a paginated table, width=800]

The list shows the most recent measurements first.

=== Treatments

The Treatments tab records all insulin-related events: bolus doses, carbohydrate entries, basal rate changes, and infusion site or sensor changes.

==== Adding a Treatment

. Click the *+ Treatments* button.
+
image::add-treatment-modal.png[Add Treatment modal with fields for treatment type, date/time, dose, and carbohydrates, width=700]
. Select the *Type*:
** *Bolus* — a manual insulin injection (enter dose in units and optionally carbs in grams).
** *Correction Bolus* — insulin given to correct a high glucose (enter dose only).
** *Basal Change* — a temporary basal rate change (enter rate and duration).
** *Site Change* — record a new infusion set insertion.
** *Sensor Insert* — record a new CGM sensor insertion.
. Set the *Recorded At* date and time.
. Fill in the relevant fields (dose, carbs, etc.).
. Click *Save*.

=== Dose Calculator

[WARNING]
====
*This is a suggested dose — always verify with your healthcare team before injecting.*

The dose calculator provides a recommendation based on your current glucose reading, CGM trend, entered carbohydrates, and active basal profile.
It is a decision-support tool, not a prescription.
====

The Dose Calc tab walks you through a structured calculation.

==== Step 1 — Enter Current Blood Glucose

image::dose-calculator.png[Dose calculator form showing BG input pre-filled from CGM, trend selector, and carbs field, width=800]

The current BG field is pre-filled from the latest CGM reading (shown as "Pre-filled from CGM reading N min ago").
If the CGM reading is more than 30 minutes old, a warning asks you to enter a fresh fingerstick value.

==== Step 2 — Select CGM Trend

Choose the arrow that matches your current CGM trend:

* Rising rapidly / Rising / Rising slowly
* Stable
* Falling slowly / Falling / Falling rapidly

The trend influences the dose calculation.

==== Step 3 — Enter Carbohydrates

Enter the carbohydrates of the meal you are about to eat (grams).
Leave at zero for a correction bolus only.

==== Step 4 — Calculate

Click *Calculate*.
The result panel shows:

image::dose-calculator-result.png[Dose calculator result showing correction dose, carb dose, trend adjustment, and total recommended dose, width=800]

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Meaning

| *Correction*
| Dose to bring glucose back to target

| *Carbs*
| Dose to cover the entered carbohydrates (based on your ICR)

| *Trend*
| Adjustment for glucose direction (reduces dose if falling, increases if rising)

| *Total*
| Sum of the above — the recommended dose

| *Your adjustment*
| A field where you can enter a manual ± adjustment (e.g. for planned activity)

| *Final dose*
| Recommended total after your adjustment
|===

[WARNING]
====
Always review the recommended dose before accepting.
If the dose seems unusually high or low, consult your healthcare team.
====

[NOTE]
====
*The dose calculation does not automatically deduct active Insulin On Board (IOB).*
Before accepting the recommended dose, check the IOB shown on your Dashboard.
If you have significant IOB remaining from a recent bolus, discuss insulin stacking risk with your healthcare team before injecting.
====

==== Step 5 — SEA Timer (Insulin-to-Meal Interval)

The SEA (*Spritz-Ess-Abstand*, or insulin-to-meal interval) shows the recommended wait time between injecting and eating.
Your active profile defines SEA segments by time of day and glucose level.
After clicking *Accept & Log*, a countdown timer starts for the recommended interval.

==== Step 6 — Accept & Log

Click *Accept & Log N units* to record the dose as a treatment entry.
The dose appears in the Treatments list.

== Part III — Analytics and Reports

This part explains the Analytics tab and the Report generator.
These features help you and your healthcare team review trends over days, weeks, or months.

=== Analytics

The Analytics tab shows aggregated statistics for the selected date range.

==== Timeframe Selector

image::analytics-timeframe.png[Timeframe selector showing date range controls at the top of the Analytics page, width=800]

Use the date-range controls at the top of the Analytics page to choose the analysis period.
Common choices are 7 days, 14 days, 30 days, or 90 days.
A longer range gives more statistically reliable estimates.

==== HbA1c Estimate

[IMPORTANT]
====
The HbA1c value shown is an *estimate* calculated from CGM data using the DCCT formula.
It is not equivalent to a laboratory HbA1c blood test.
Always confirm with your healthcare team using a certified lab result.
====

image::analytics-hba1c.png[HbA1c estimate card showing percentage value with colour-coded target bands, width=600]

The HbA1c estimate is derived from your CGM mean glucose using:

 eHbA1c (%) = (mean glucose mg/dL + 46.7) / 28.7

Source: DCCT (Diabetes Control and Complications Trial).

The ADA target for most T1D adults is *< 7%* (< 53 mmol/mol).

==== Time In Range (TIR)

image::analytics-tir.png[Time In Range bar showing five glucose zones coloured from very low to very high, width=800]

TIR shows what percentage of your CGM readings fell in each glucose zone during the selected period.

[cols="1,1,1,1", options="header"]
|===
| Zone | mg/dL | mmol/L | Clinical target

| *Very Low* (dark red)
| < 54
| < 3.0
| < 1%

| *Low* (red)
| < 70 (includes Very Low)
| < 3.9 (includes Very Low)
| < 4% (cumulative below 70)

| *Target — In Range* (green)
| 70–180
| 3.9–10.0
| *≥ 70%*

| *High* (yellow/amber)
| 180–250
| 10.0–13.9
| < 25%

| *Very High* (orange)
| > 250
| > 13.9
| < 5%
|===

Clinical targets per ATTD/ADA international consensus for most T1D adults.

==== AGP — Ambulatory Glucose Profile

image::analytics-agp.png[AGP chart showing median line and percentile bands over a 24-hour period, width=800]

The Ambulatory Glucose Profile (AGP) overlays all days in the selected period onto a single 24-hour axis.
It shows:

* *Median* (50th percentile) — the middle line; the "average day".
* *Interquartile range* (25th–75th percentiles) — where half your readings fell; a wide band indicates high variability.
* *10th–90th percentile range* — the outer shaded area; extreme readings.

A narrow IQR band indicates consistent glucose control.
A wide band indicates high day-to-day variability — discuss with your healthcare team.

The AGP follows the international AGP report standard (Dexcom/Agamatrix consensus, Ambulatory Glucose Profile 2013).

[NOTE]
====
The AGP is most meaningful when based on at least 14 days of CGM data with ≥ 70% sensor wear time.
====

==== Basal and Bolus Averages

image::analytics-basal-avg.png[Basal rate average chart showing mean scheduled basal insulin by hour of day, width=800]

image::analytics-bolus-avg.png[Bolus average chart showing mean bolus insulin by hour of day, width=800]

These charts show the average insulin delivery broken down by hour of day over the selected period.
Peaks in the bolus average typically correspond to meal times.
Compare the basal average against your profile schedule to spot persistent over- or under-delivery.

=== Report

The Report tab generates a printable, multi-page PDF of your glucose data.

==== Setting the Date Range

image::report-date-range.png[Report page showing date range controls with preset buttons and custom date fields, width=800]

Use the preset buttons (Last 7 / 14 / 30 / 90 days) or enter a custom *From* / *To* date.

[NOTE]
====
The AGP chart requires at least 14 days of data to be statistically meaningful.
Very large date ranges (> 90 days) may take longer to generate.
====

==== Selecting Report Pages

image::report-page-selection.png[Page selection panel showing checkboxes for all 11 report sub-pages, width=800]

The page selection panel lets you choose which sub-pages to include.
Use *Select All* / *Deselect All* to toggle quickly.
The number of deselected pages is shown in the button label.

The available sub-pages are:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Sub-page | Contents

| *Summary Report* (AUSWERTUNG)
| Patient info, days analysed, CGM reading count, insulin types, device change counts, TIR bars, glucose statistics (min/max/mean/SD), daily averages

| *Summary*
| Compact TIR + glucose statistics overview

| *AGP Percentile Chart*
| AGP chart with sensor wear days and TIR summary

| *Daily Statistics*
| Per-day table: reading count, TIR bands (%), median, P25/P75, eHbA1c

| *Daily Trend*
| Hour × day heat-map coloured by glucose zone, with carb indicators

| *Weekly Overlay Chart*
| All days' glucose curves overlaid on a 24-hour axis, coloured by calendar week

| *Daily Charts*
| One CGM trace per day with bolus/carb/event markers (most recent 14 days)

| *Glucose Distribution*
| Histogram of CGM readings by glucose zone; zone percentages

| *Basal Profile*
| Active profile basal schedule, ICR, ISF, and glucose targets

| *CGP* (Comprehensive Glucose Pentagon)
| Radar chart comparing your values against reference on 5 axes: Time on Target, Variability, Hypoglycaemia Intensity, Hyperglycaemia Intensity, Mean Glucose. PGR score shown. Source: Vigersky & McMahon (2018).

| *Basal Rate Chart* (BASAL_RATE)
| 24-hour basal schedule as table and bar chart; total daily basal insulin
|===

[NOTE]
====
The report is generated server-side from data at the moment of generation.
If you add new treatments after generating, click *Generate* again to refresh the report.
====

==== Generating and Downloading the PDF

image::report-generated.png[Generated report view showing the first report page, width=800]

. Set the date range.
. Select the pages you want.
. Click *Generate Report*.
. When the report appears in the preview pane, click *Download PDF* to save it.

Share the PDF with your healthcare team at your next appointment.

== Part IV — Settings, Profiles, and Integrations

This part explains how to configure your account preferences, manage insulin profiles, and connect external devices.

=== Settings

image::settings-form.png[Settings form showing timezone, language, time format, glucose unit, weight unit, sensor lifespan, and alarm threshold fields, width=800]

Open the *Settings* tab to configure your personal preferences.

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Field | Description

| *Timezone*
| Your local IANA timezone (e.g. `Europe/Berlin`, `America/New_York`). Used for all timestamps.

| *Language*
| Interface language. Currently English (EN) and German (DE) are available.

| *Time format*
| 12-hour or 24-hour clock display.

| *Glucose unit*
| mg/dL (US/conventional) or mmol/L (international). Affects all glucose values in the UI.

| *Weight unit*
| kg or lbs. Affects weight measurement entries.

| *Sensor lifespan (hours)*
| Expected CGM sensor duration. Common values: Libre 3/G7 = 336 h, G6/Guardian 4 = 168 h.

| *Alarm thresholds*
| The glucose levels at which alerts are shown (Urgent High, High, Low, Urgent Low).
|===

[IMPORTANT]
====
*Glucose unit and timezone changes take effect on your next login.*

After saving either of these settings, log out and log in again for the change to apply fully.
The interface shows a note ("changes will take effect on your next login") after saving.
====

==== Alarm Threshold Rules

image::settings-alarm-error.png[Settings form showing a validation error when alarm thresholds are in the wrong order, width=800]

The four alarm thresholds must satisfy:

 Urgent Low < Low < High < Urgent High

kdiab enforces this order and shows a validation error if you enter conflicting values.
For example, entering an Urgent Low value that is higher than the Low value triggers the error shown above.

=== Profiles

A basal profile defines your scheduled basal insulin delivery rates, insulin-to-carb ratio (ICR), insulin sensitivity factor (ISF), and glucose targets — each split into time segments throughout the day.

[TIP]
====
Profiles are created and adjusted in collaboration with your healthcare team.
Do not change basal rates, ICR, or ISF without guidance from your endocrinologist.
====

==== Profile List

image::profile-list.png[Profile list showing an active profile and archived profiles, width=800]

The Profiles tab shows all your profiles:

* *Active* — the profile currently used for dose calculations and report generation.
* *Archived* — previous versions kept for historical reference.

Click a profile name to view its full schedule (basal rates, ICR, ISF, targets, SEA segments).

==== Profile Editor

image::profile-editor.png[Profile editor showing time-segmented basal rates, ICR, ISF, and target range fields, width=800]

The profile editor shows each field broken into time segments covering 24 hours.
Hover over a field label to see a tooltip explaining typical clinical values:

* *Basal rate* — units of insulin per hour (typical: 0.2–1.5 U/h)
* *ICR* — grams of carbs covered by 1 unit (typical: 1:8 to 1:20)
* *ISF* — how much 1 unit lowers glucose (typical: 30–80 mg/dL per unit)
* *Targets* — your goal glucose range (ADA recommends 80–130 mg/dL pre-meal for most adults)

==== Profile History and Copy-on-Write

image::profile-history.png[Profile history showing a list of previous profile versions with dates, width=800]

Every change to a profile creates a new version — the old version is archived, not deleted.
This *copy-on-write* model means your historical data always reflects the profile that was active at the time.

==== Proposed Profile (Doctor-Initiated Change)

image::profile-diff.png[Profile diff view showing differences between the current active profile and a proposed change, width=800]

When your doctor proposes a profile change, a *Proposed* badge appears on the Profiles tab.
Click the badge to open the diff view, which highlights exactly what changed (e.g. a modified basal rate segment).

[NOTE]
====
Only accept a proposed profile after discussing the changes with your treating physician.
Accepting activates the new profile immediately and it will be used for all future dose calculations.
====

You can *Accept* to activate the proposed profile, or *Reject* to keep the current one.

=== Nightscout Device Integration

[[_nightscout_device_integration]]

kdiab includes an optional Nightscout API v1 compatibility layer.
This allows APS (Automated Pump Systems) and CGM apps to push data into kdiab using the same protocol they already use for Nightscout.

[NOTE]
====
Nightscout integration is optional and must be enabled by your clinic administrator.
If the Device Status widget on your Dashboard shows no data, ask your administrator to activate the Nightscout endpoint for your account.
====

==== Supported Devices

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| App | Integration direction

| *AAPS* (AndroidAPS)
| Pushes CGM readings, treatments (bolus, basal, carbs), device status → kdiab

| *xDrip+*
| Pushes CGM readings → kdiab

| *Juggluco*
| Pushes CGM readings → kdiab
|===

==== Connection Settings

Configure your device app with these settings:

[cols="1,3", options="header"]
|===
| Setting | Value

| *Nightscout base URL*
| `http(s)://<kdiab-host>/api/v1`

| *API Secret*
| Your personal API token (provided by your administrator)
|===

Data flow:

* *Device → kdiab*: CGM readings, bolus/basal/carb events, device status (reservoir, battery, sensor age)
* *kdiab → device*: profile data (basal rates) for APS loop calculations (read via `/api/v1/profile`)

== Glossary

[[AGP]]
=== AGP — Ambulatory Glucose Profile
A standardised chart that overlays all CGM readings onto a single 24-hour axis, showing median and percentile bands.
Used by clinicians to spot patterns in glucose variability.

[[HbA1c]]
=== HbA1c — Glycated Haemoglobin
A blood test reflecting average glucose over the preceding ~3 months.
Expressed as a percentage (NGSP/DCCT scale) or mmol/mol (IFCC scale).
kdiab shows an *estimated* HbA1c computed from CGM data (not a lab test).

[[TIR]]
=== TIR — Time In Range
The percentage of CGM readings within the clinical target range (70–180 mg/dL / 3.9–10.0 mmol/L).
International consensus target: ≥ 70% for most T1D adults.

[[CGM]]
=== CGM — Continuous Glucose Monitor
A sensor worn on the body that measures interstitial glucose every 1–5 minutes and transmits readings wirelessly (e.g. Dexcom G6/G7, FreeStyle Libre 3).

[[BGM]]
=== BGM — Blood Glucose Monitor
A traditional fingerstick meter producing a single glucose reading per test.
More accurate than CGM but less frequent.

[[IOB]]
=== IOB — Insulin On Board
The estimated amount of active insulin remaining from previous bolus doses, based on the Duration of Insulin Action (DIA) curve.

[[COB]]
=== COB — Carbs On Board
The estimated active carbohydrates remaining from previous meal entries, based on carb absorption rate.

[[ISF]]
=== ISF — Insulin Sensitivity Factor
How much one unit of insulin lowers blood glucose (typical: 30–80 mg/dL per unit).
Used by the dose calculator to compute the correction dose.

[[ICR]]
=== ICR — Insulin-to-Carb Ratio
How many grams of carbohydrates one unit of insulin covers (typical: 1:8 to 1:20).
Used by the dose calculator to compute the meal dose.

[[DIA]]
=== DIA — Duration of Insulin Action
The time (in hours) over which a bolus dose of insulin is active.
Affects IOB calculations.
Defined in the active basal profile.

[[SEA]]
=== SEA — Spritz-Ess-Abstand
German for "insulin-to-meal interval" — the recommended wait time in minutes between injecting insulin and starting to eat.
Defined as time-segmented entries in the basal profile.

[[Basal]]
=== Basal Insulin
Background insulin delivered continuously to maintain stable glucose between meals.
In pump therapy, the basal rate (U/h) is defined in time segments and can be temporarily increased, decreased, or suspended.

[[Bolus]]
=== Bolus Insulin
A discrete dose of insulin delivered at a meal or to correct a high glucose reading.
Logged as a treatment event in kdiab.

[[CGP]]
=== CGP — Comprehensive Glucose Pentagon
A radar chart scoring glucose control on five clinical axes: Time on Target, Variability, Hypoglycaemia Intensity, Hyperglycaemia Intensity, and Mean Glucose.
Introduced by Vigersky & McMahon (2018).
The PGR (Percentage of readings in range) score summarises the pentagon as a single number.

[[PGR]]
=== PGR — Percentage of Glucose Readings in Range
A single number derived from the CGP pentagon, summarising overall glucose control.

[[AAPS]]
=== AAPS — AndroidAPS
An open-source Automated Pump System (APS) app for Android that uses a control algorithm to automate insulin delivery based on CGM readings and a basal profile.
Connects to kdiab via the Nightscout v1 API.
